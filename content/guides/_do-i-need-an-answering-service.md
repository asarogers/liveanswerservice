You likely need an answering service if you regularly miss calls, send callers to voicemail after hours, or work in the field where you can't answer the phone — and those missed calls are real customers you're losing to a competitor who picked up. If your business depends on inbound phone calls and you can't personally answer all of them at all hours, an answering service usually pays for itself fast. If your call volume is low, predictable, and fully covered by you or a front desk, you probably don't need one yet.

That's the short answer. Here's how to know which side of the line you're on.

## The signs you need one

**You're missing calls.** This is the big one, and most owners underestimate it. Pull your call log and count the unanswered inbound calls over the last 30 days. For a home-services or medical business, a missed call is frequently a missed job worth hundreds or thousands of dollars. If you're missing even a few a week, the math gets ugly quickly.

**Callers hit voicemail — and don't leave one.** Most people don't leave voicemails anymore; they call the next business on the list. If your after-hours plan is voicemail, you're effectively closed to every caller who won't wait until morning.

**You're a solo operator or you work in the field.** A plumber under a sink, a roofer on a ladder, or an agent in a showing physically cannot answer the phone. Every one of those calls is a coin flip on whether the lead survives. An answering service makes it a guarantee.

**You have after-hours and weekend gaps.** Emergencies don't keep business hours. An HVAC failure on a Saturday night or a personal-injury inquiry at 9 PM is exactly the high-value call you can't afford to drop.

**You're growing faster than your front desk.** When call volume outpaces the person answering the phone, calls start queuing, getting dropped, or pulling your team off revenue work to play receptionist.

**You want bookings, not just messages.** If you need appointments on the calendar and leads in your CRM — not a pile of "call back" notes — a modern answering service that books and qualifies is the upgrade you want.

## A quick self-assessment

Answer yes or no:

1. Do you depend on inbound phone calls for new customers?
2. In the last month, did any calls go unanswered or to voicemail?
3. Do calls come in when you're closed, asleep, or working in the field?
4. Would a same-day booking from an after-hours call be worth real money to you?
5. Do you serve Spanish-speaking customers you sometimes can't help on the phone?

Three or more "yes" answers means an answering service is very likely worth it. Four or five, and you're probably already losing money by not having one.

## When you DON'T need one

An answering service isn't for everyone. You can skip it if:

- Your call volume is low and you (or a front desk) genuinely answer every call live.
- Your business runs almost entirely on web forms, email, or online booking, and phone calls are rare.
- You don't take after-hours calls and your customers don't expect you to.

If that's you, save the money — but revisit it the moment you start missing calls or notice after-hours gaps. Most owners cross that line as they grow without noticing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if I'm missing enough calls to justify an answering service?

Pull your phone's call log and count unanswered inbound calls over the last 30 days, then multiply by your average job or customer value. For home services, medical, and legal businesses, even a handful of missed calls a month usually exceeds the cost of an answering service, because each missed call is often a lost customer who simply called the next business.

### Is an answering service worth it for a solo operator?

Often yes — solo operators are the clearest case. If you're in the field, on a ladder, or with a client, you physically can't answer the phone, so every call is at risk. An answering service that books appointments and captures leads while you work essentially gives a one-person business a full-time front desk for a flat monthly fee.

### Do I need an answering service or just voicemail?

Voicemail loses most callers, because the majority won't leave a message — they call a competitor instead. An answering service captures those calls live, books appointments, and qualifies leads, which voicemail can't do. If your callers are potential customers comparison-shopping, voicemail is effectively a closed sign.
