## Nutrition targets during recovery (older adults)

- **Protein target during recovery: 1.2–1.5g per kg body weight per day** — significantly above baseline. For a 150-lb (68 kg) person, ~80–100g/day. That's roughly: four eggs + a 4-oz salmon fillet + a cup of Greek yogurt in one day.
- **Vitamin C** — collagen synthesis and wound healing. Sources: bell peppers (more C than citrus), broccoli, citrus, strawberries, tomatoes.
- **Zinc** — immune response, tissue repair. Sources: meat, poultry, beans, pumpkin seeds.
- **Calcium and Vitamin D** — critical specifically after orthopedic procedures (hip, knee, fracture repair). Sources: dairy, fortified plant milks, salmon, eggs.
- **Iron** — replaces blood lost in surgery. Sources: lean red meat, beans, leafy greens. Pair plant iron with a vitamin C source (spinach alone absorbs poorly).
- **Hydration** — anesthesia, pain meds, and reduced mobility all dehydrate. Small frequent sips of water, broth, electrolyte drinks throughout the day, not just at meals.

## Texture and tolerance by surgery type

**Abdominal / digestive surgery** (colon resection, hernia repair, gallbladder): clear liquids → soft foods → regular as tolerated. Avoid high-fiber foods early. Easy-to-digest proteins: eggs, yogurt, fish, soft tofu, well-cooked chicken. No raw vegetables or whole grains for at least the first week.

**Cardiac surgery** (bypass, valve replacement): heart-healthy diet starts immediately. Sodium under 2,000mg/day, limited saturated fat, high fruit/veg. Reduced appetite is common for weeks — strategy is calorie-dense small portions.

**Orthopedic surgery** (hip, knee, spinal): no texture restriction; the practical challenge is mobility. Person may not be able to stand at the stove, reach the microwave, or carry a full bowl safely. Everything in single-serve containers at accessible counter height. High protein, bone-supporting nutrients, easy reheating.

**Oral / jaw surgery**: soft and pureed only per surgeon. Smoothies, yogurt, mashed potatoes, pureed soups, scrambled eggs, soft fish.

## Week 1 prep (soft, simple, high-protein)

- **Scrambled egg packs:** 2-egg portions in microwave-safe containers, reheat 45 sec, 12g protein. Make 6–8.
- **Greek yogurt (full-fat, not low-fat):** higher protein and calories. Stock 7–10 individual containers. 15–17g protein per cup.
- **Bone broth:** collagen, easy to sip, comforting when appetite is suppressed. Mug mid-morning and mid-afternoon — hydration plus healing nutrients.
- **Soft-baked salmon:** olive oil, lemon, dill. 425°F for 12–14 min. Stores 3–4 days. Flakes with no chewing effort. 25g protein per 4-oz serving.
- **Mashed sweet potato:** bake/microwave 4–5, mash with butter and salt, portion individually. Vitamin C, potassium, easy calories.
- **Chicken soup:** large pot with shredded (not cubed — easier to eat) chicken, soft vegetables, low-sodium broth. Freeze single-serving. MVP of weeks 1–2.

## Week 2 prep (more variety, increasing protein)

- **Sheet pan meals:** chicken thighs or salmon with roasted zucchini, bell peppers, broccoli — soft when roasted through, season well (flavor stimulates appetite).
- **Bean soups / lentil dishes:** high protein, high fiber (once digestive patients are cleared), easy reheat.
- **Soft grain bowls:** quinoa or brown rice + roasted veg + protein. Eaten warm or room temp — useful when person is eating in chair or in bed.
- **Protein smoothies:** full-fat Greek yogurt + banana + frozen berries + handful spinach + scoop protein powder. 25–35g protein, 350–500 cal in a glass — goes down when nothing else will.

## Kitchen setup before discharge

- **Move essentials to counter level.** Reaching overhead is unsafe after shoulder, spinal, or cardiac surgery. Move plates, glasses, snacks before discharge day.
- **Bedside mini fridge / cooler** if recovery is bedroom-bound: yogurt, pre-cut soft fruit, cheese sticks, water. Nourishment shouldn't require navigating the kitchen at 2am on pain meds.
- **Pre-portion everything.** Full containers require decision-making. Individual portions are grab-and-heat. Label every container with reheat instructions ("2 min microwave, stir once") so any helper can use them.
- **Mobility hazards:** if walker/crutches, no rugs in kitchen-to-table path, nothing to carry while balancing.

## Distance / handoff caregiving

When you can't be there every day, prepare a one-page food guide: what's in the fridge, what's in the freezer, how to reheat each item, dietary restrictions, what to watch for (poor intake, swelling, signs of infection, significant weight loss).

In-home meal prep service 2–3x/week beats generic meal delivery: a delivery subscription doesn't know your parent had a hip replacement last Tuesday and needs high-protein soft foods, not a meal kit that requires 30 min of standing at the stove.

## FAQ-style answers worth keeping

- **Protein after surgery:** 1.2–1.5g/kg/day, ~80–100g/day for a 150-lb person. Easy sources post-op: Greek yogurt (15–17g/cup), salmon (25g/4oz), scrambled eggs (12g/2-egg), bone broth, soft-cooked chicken.
- **Best soft foods to prep after hip replacement:** mashed sweet potato (portioned), shredded chicken thighs, soft-baked salmon, scrambled egg packs, Greek yogurt, single-serve chicken soup, quinoa/brown rice grain bowls. All pre-portioned, all labeled with reheat instructions.
- **How long modified foods are needed:**
  - Abdominal/digestive: liquids → soft → regular over 2–4 weeks per surgeon.
  - Orthopedic: typically no texture restriction — the issue is access, not diet.
  - Cardiac: dietary changes (low sodium, low sat fat) generally permanent, not temporary.
  - Oral: soft/pureed until surgeon clears, typically 2–6 weeks.
- **Why first 2–4 weeks matter most:** nutrition is highest-leverage for healing exactly when family caregiver is most stretched (medical appointments, meds, work, family).
