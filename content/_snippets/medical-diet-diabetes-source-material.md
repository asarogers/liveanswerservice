## Carbohydrate framework

Not all carbs hit blood sugar the same way. High-fiber carbs (vegetables, legumes, whole grains) digest slowly — gentle gradual rise. Refined carbs (white bread, white rice, sugary drinks, processed snacks) spike fast.

**Diabetes-friendly plate:** 50% non-starchy vegetables, 25% lean protein, 25% complex carbohydrate. This structure naturally keeps carb load in range without gram-counting.

**Standard guidance:** 45–60g carbohydrate per meal for most adults with type 2 diabetes — but individual targets vary based on medications, activity level, insulin use, and specific type. Always defer to the healthcare team / RD for the specific number. Justine's role is the kitchen side: planning, shopping, cooking, setup — so the medical team's guidance actually gets executed at every meal.

## Best proteins for diabetes meal prep

- Baked chicken thighs or breast (thighs reheat better — breast dries out).
- Baked salmon or tilapia.
- Hard-boiled eggs — cook 10–12 at a time, peel immediately.
- Ground turkey browned with onion, garlic, no-salt-added tomato.
- Canned tuna or salmon — zero prep, 20–25g protein per serving.
- Cooked lentils or black beans — plant protein + fiber.

**Batch chicken thighs:** 6–8 bone-in thighs, olive oil, garlic, dried herbs, black pepper. 400°F for 35–40 min, internal 165°F. Pull from bone, shred or cube. 4–5 days fridge. Freezes well.

**Batch salmon:** sheet pan with foil, olive oil, lemon, dill, pinch salt. 425°F for 12–14 min. Portion individually. 3–4 days fridge. Omega-3s also support insulin sensitivity.

## Non-starchy vegetable prep

When non-starchy veg is washed and ready to go, it becomes the default option — exactly what diabetes management needs.

- Full head of broccoli into florets. Roast half at 400°F for 20 min with olive oil and garlic; keep rest raw for salads.
- Sliced bell peppers (red/yellow/orange). Some raw for snacking, sauté the rest with onion. (Bell peppers also have more vitamin C than citrus — useful for seniors on iron supplements.)
- Large container washed mixed greens / spinach — instant salad base.
- Sheet pan zucchini + cherry tomatoes roasted at 425°F for 15 min.
- Green beans steamed or roasted.

**Storage trick:** raw prepped veg keeps best in containers lined with a paper towel (absorbs moisture, extends freshness 1–2 days). Roasted veg reheats in 60–90 sec microwave.

## Complex carb bases

- **Quinoa:** 2 cups dry → ~6 cups cooked. 15-min cook. 5–6 days fridge. ~8g protein per cup cooked — one of few grains that materially contributes to protein goals.
- **Brown rice:** 2 cups dry → ~5 cups cooked. 45 min (rice cooker = set and forget). 5 days fridge. Lower GI than white rice, more fiber.
- **Sweet potato:** bake 4–6 whole at 400°F for 45–60 min. Wash and poke holes — no other prep. 5 days fridge. Reheats in 2 min. High fiber, potassium, vitamin C. Moderate GI when whole (lower than mashed).
- **Lentils:** 1.5 cups dry → ~4 cups cooked. 25–30 min. One of the lowest-GI carb sources. 9g protein per ½ cup cooked.

## Sample portioned containers

**Lunch:**
- 4 oz baked chicken or salmon
- 1 cup roasted broccoli + bell peppers
- ½ cup cooked quinoa
- Olive oil + lemon dressing on the side

**Dinner:**
- 4 oz ground turkey
- ¾ cup roasted zucchini + cherry tomatoes
- ½ cup cooked sweet potato

## Storage / safety

- Cooked proteins: 4–5 days at ≤40°F. Freeze half if prepping a full 7 days; move freezer→fridge on day 3 or 4.
- Cooked grains: 5–6 days.
- Roasted vegetables: 3–4 days at best quality; safe at 5 but texture declines.
- Label everything with prep date — masking tape + marker. Don't trust memory.
- Many household fridges drift warmer than their setting — verify with a thermometer.

## One-prep weekly pattern (10 meals from one afternoon)

- **Mon–Wed lunches:** grilled chicken thigh + roasted broccoli/bell peppers + ½ cup quinoa + lemon-herb dressing
- **Mon–Wed dinners:** baked salmon + sautéed bell peppers/zucchini + ½ baked sweet potato
- **Thu–Fri lunches:** ground turkey over brown rice + roasted cherry tomatoes + green beans
- **Thu–Fri dinners:** lentil soup (30 min cook on Thursday) + large green salad

## FAQ-style answers worth keeping

- **Best foods to avoid:** refined carbs (white rice, white bread, sugary sauces, sweetened yogurts, processed snacks). Watch hidden sugar in condiments, dressings, canned goods. Read labels for total carbs and fiber — net carbs (total minus fiber) is what hits blood sugar.
- **Sweet potato — yes, in portion:** medium sweet potato ≈ 23g carb, 4g fiber. Whole (not mashed) has moderate GI. Half sweet potato + protein + non-starchy veg fits 45–60g target. One of the most nutrient-dense carb options in the rotation.
- **Diabetes + low appetite:** prioritize calorie density within the blood-sugar-stable frame. Full-fat Greek yogurt (more cal + protein than low-fat), salmon, avocado, olive oil — calories without glucose spike. 5–6 small meals beats 3 large when appetite is patchy. Pre-portion so eating requires zero effort. Persistent low appetite affecting weight → talk to the doctor; often a medication side effect with adjustable options.
